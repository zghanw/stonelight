Field Capture Guide: How to Shoot at the Stone

1

Prop the Phone Still

Do not hold the phone by hand between shots. Prop it on a beanbag, stone, or ground stick facing the inscription. Every photo must cover the exact same view.

2

Lock Camera Exposure (AE/AF)

Tap and hold the phone screen on the carving until AE/AF Lock appears. This stops the camera from brightening shadows or changing focus between torch angles.

3

Take One Flash Photo

Take one flat flash or sunlight photo first to capture overall stone tone and layout as a baseline.

4

Sweep the Torch at 6–8 Clock Points

Turn on a torch or phone light. Hold it 30–60 cm away at a low grazing angle (15° to 20° above the stone surface). Take a shot at 12:00, 2:00, 4:00, 6:00, 8:00, and 10:00.

Preservation Ethics: Never Touch or Harm Historic Stones

Never apply shaving cream, chalk, flour, wire brushes, or harsh chemicals to stones. Raking light works purely through physics and optical shadows, leaving fragile headstones unharmed.
