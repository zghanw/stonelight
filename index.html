/ 01 The problem

Flash flattens the shadows you need.

A carved letter is only a shallow groove. Light it head-on and the groove disappears. Light it from the side and every stroke throws a shadow. Stonemasons call it raking light.

  • Shaving cream, chalk, flour. They speed up the stone's decay.
  • Wire brushes. They take the surface with them.
  • Asking strangers to enhance a photo. The detail was never captured.

/ 02 How it works

Four photos is enough.
Eight is better.

  1. 
                Step 01
                
    Prop your phone. Take one flat photo.
    
                
    Lean the phone on a bag or a stick so it cannot move. This flat shot is the baseline, and it usually shows almost nothing.
    
              
    2. 
              
  2. 
                Step 02
                
    Walk a torch around the stone.
    
                
    Hold it low, almost skimming the surface, and take a photo at each clock position. A second phone works as the torch.
    
              
    3. 
              
  3. 
                Step 03
                
    Stonelight works out the surface.
    
                
    Each pixel is brighter when the torch faces its slope. Eight readings are enough to solve which way every pixel leans. It all runs in your browser.
    
                
    I = L · g
g = (LᵀL)⁻¹ Lᵀ I
shade = 1 + (g · d) × rake
    
              
    4. 
              
  4. 
                Step 04
                
    Now the light is yours.
    
                
    Drag it anywhere and lower it until the strokes appear. Keep scrolling to sweep it around the stone.
    
              
    5. 
              
  5. 
                Step 05
                
    Type what you can read.
    
                
    Use a dash for letters that are gone. Print the record, or copy it straight into Find a Grave.
